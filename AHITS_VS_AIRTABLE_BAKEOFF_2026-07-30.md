## 0 · The ruling

**Keep AHITS as the system of record. Ship a one-way Airtable read layer within three weeks. Decline all three migrations. Two orders ride the ruling: close the CC-30 live-fire boxes this week, and put a named second maintainer in place before CC-17's first money line.**

The single decisive factor, in the Judge's sentence: *the offline zero-lost-writes MUSTs are physics imposed by this fleet's signal reality and only the incumbent meets them — while both drivers the stakeholders actually named are fully purchasable for $0–4k and roughly two weeks without conceding a single register row.*

Scorecard totals (of 28 register MUSTs): **AHITS ≈27–27.5 · full Airtable ≈5 · AppSheet ≈16 · Fulcrum stack ≈11.5 · Hybrid (AHITS capture + Airtable read layer) ≈27.5 with both named drivers won.** The interrogation was not close on the register. It is uncomfortably close on the bus factor — and that is fixed by calendar discipline, not by Airtable (§8).

## 3 · The champions' best cases (steelmanned; full designs in the appendix)

### Airtable (Team/Business/Enterprise Scale, current stack as of 2026-07-31: Interfaces, Forms, Automations, native mobile app + barcode field, Portals add-on, Web API, Omni AI)

The strongest honest Airtable design leans everything on the existing company plan: one base as system of record, Interfaces per persona, the native mobile app's barcode field for scans, Forms + prefilled links standing in for the portal, ~40 automations for the alert engine, and Airtable's genuine crown — self-serve manager views/reports (R-ADM-2 scored ✓✓, the only double-win on the board). The champion did not flinch on the flank it was ordered not to flinch on: **Airtable officially does not support offline access on any platform**, so the design's field fallback is paper day-cards typed in later. Build to best-achievable approximation: **~10–16 weeks — and it concedes nine MUSTs including all seven offline rows, PIN auth, GPS evidence, and the tokenized portal.**

## 4 · Where each path dies — the Field Antagonist

# Field Antagonist — where each path actually dies

**Airtable (full replacement): dies at the field edge, before breakfast.** The vendor's own support page says offline access is not supported ([technical requirements](https://support.airtable.com/docs/what-are-the-technical-requirements-for-using-airtable), fetched 2026-07-31); the corpus says lie-fi is the *normal* condition and that a hanging submit trains operators to swipe-kill the app. That single collision kills R-OFF-1..7, the lie-fi clause of R-FRIC-1, offline QR, and (separately, via the missing location field) the passive-GPS evidence lane — nine MUSTs. The champion knows it; their fallback is paper plus later typed entry, i.e., the incumbent AHITS was built to beat. Fully alive as the read-only reporting layer — which is what PRD §9.3 planned all along.**

**AppSheet (Enterprise Plus on Cloud SQL): the only challenger that actually shows up to the offline fight — and it dies on the letter of the charter, then bleeds from four cuts.** The queue is real, durable, and documented-idempotent; local-first save genuinely deletes the lie-fi hang. **Fulcrum stack: dies on arithmetic and scope, and the champion says so first.** Superb native capture; then no money loop (D19 unexecuted — it *retains* Clockify's invoicing, the recorded felt pain, as a paid subscription), no Today (the static menu D5 refused to launch on), no invariants, no enforced two-party transfer, no portal, and a glue service that relocates the alert engine and consistency problem onto the same single maintainer across three stores and four consoles. ## Dry reading
1. **There is no cheap door.** At an honest labor rate every path lands in six figures over 24 months. The decision is not build-vs-buy on price; it is *whose labor, what it purchases, and which spend is reversible*.
2. **B does not undercut A even on full cost.** The Airtable migration's band ($72k–$131k) overlaps AHITS-as-is ($82k–$131k) — roughly equal money for a register with every offline MUST conceded. The only frame where B "saves money" is cash-only with the build done by Max priced at zero — the identical free-labor assumption that makes A nearly free ($6.5k–$10.5k cash). Stakeholders must not be allowed to price Max at $0 in one column and market rate in the other.
3. **No challenger deletes the build; they relocate it.** B needs 400–640 builder-hours to reach approximation-with-concessions; C needs 480–680 plus a permanent config owner; D's "buy" contains a 4–8 week from-scratch REBUILD of AHITS's alert/invariant engine as a glue service. 4. **AHITS is the only seat-invariant path.** Marginal cost per operator-year: Airtable Business ≈ $324, AppSheet ≈ $240 assigned (≈$25/season if User Pass verifies), Fulcrum Elite ≈ $726 with Clockify. At the 90-operator target the challengers carry $23k–$69k/yr of pure seat spend forever; AHITS's total cash — including PITR and the Claude subscription — is less than the price of eight Fulcrum seats.
5. **Seasonality, not churn, is the seat-bill sting.** All three vendors reuse seats on churn; the cost driver is peak headcount. Fulcrum is worst: annual commitment priced at seasonal peak, monthly billing dies after year 1, and the pricing page states the vendor will "learn your usage pattern and offer appropriate pricing with an annual commitment" — a sourced repricing mechanism at renewal [L18]. Airtable monthly billing is the churn-shaped choice at +20% over annual list.
6. **The caps are deferred price rises.** Airtable Team structurally fails this workload (50k records/base, ~25k automation runs, 100k API calls shared across the whole company workspace) — so the real Airtable quote is Business ($54/seat monthly) or Enterprise (unpublished). Two challenger quotes hang on unpublished prices (Airtable Enterprise Scale; AppSheet User Pass), and one on an ambiguous pricing page (Fulcrum Elite $55-vs-$39 toggle). 7. **A's two honestly-unbooked items:** (a) PITR $100/mo — the backup/restore MUST is still an unchecked box as of 07-30, and the add-on costs 4× the Supabase base fee; book it now [L2][L26]; (b) production standup if D1 ever un-defers (+$1.5k–$3k). Both are noise at scenario scale — and the SaaS paths get backups by construction, the one structural cost point in their favor.
8. **The lost-write incident is the asymmetric cost.** On A it is an engineering discipline already paid for (CC-29/CC-30). On B it is structural — no offline base exists; at 30 operators × ~120 field days, even a 0.5% capture-day failure rate ≈ 18 charter P0s per season ≈ $3.6k–$10.8k in incident labor *before* the first custody or pay dispute [L29]. ## The hybrid, one level deeper (the winning path)

**Mechanics.** A nightly (later hourly) cron on the existing Cloud Run reads AHITS Postgres and upserts ~12 denormalized read-model tables into one Airtable base ("AHITS Reporting") via the Web API, keyed on a locked `ahits_id` field: checks_flat, custody_ledger, deployments, fleet_status (vehicles + maintenance nextDue + insurance/registration), inventory_positions, requests_lines, open/resolved alerts, maintenance_tasks, pilot_metrics_daily (pre-aggregated in SQL so metric logic never forks) — post-CC-17 adds time_weekly rollups and invoice status. Cost/rate columns land only in a manager-restricted table, and since operators never touch Airtable at all, the operator-cost wall (R-ADM-1) holds trivially — sidestepping the undocumented interface/PAT boundary entirely. Photos sync as thumbnails or link-outs to AHITS's signed viewer (Airtable attachment URLs expire). **Direction is strictly one-way:** Airtable-side edits live in designated annotation fields the sync never reads; write-backs are denied by a standing decision appended to DECISIONS.md. The sync stamps per-table freshness and raises a sync-age >26h alert through AHITS's existing engine — the sync gets its own dead-man.

**What it makes possible.** Stewart (admin #2, UK) and the UK parent get self-serve views, filters, charts, CSV, and shared read-only links inside the Airtable plan they already administer, with zero Max in the loop per view; the CC-18 week board lands as a timeline interface over deployments × days (maintenance nextDue, request neededBy, rental windows, missed-check flags) this quarter instead of Q1-2027, with its falsifier ("does the ops lead's Monday start here?") cheaply testable; and it generates live evidence about manager Airtable adoption before anyone re-litigates a fuller migration.

**Cost.** 40–80 hrs build + 2–4 hrs/mo schema-drift care (add a CI check that fails when a Prisma migration touches a synced column without a mapping update). Cash $0–$4k/24mo: 2–3 manager editor seats only if the company plan lacks headroom; read-only consumers are free. Zero operator-side change, zero retraining, zero register concessions, no offline re-verification — no write path moves.
